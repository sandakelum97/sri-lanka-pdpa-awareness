# Draft Instrument Summaries (consultation drafts — NOT LAW)

Version 1.0 · 31 Oct 2026 · Source: official PDFs on https://www.dpa.gov.lk/guidelines.php, read in full.

Every instrument below is a Draft 1.0 published by the Data Protection Authority in September–October 2024 for public consultation. None has a number, date, Minister's signature, Gazette publication or Parliamentary approval. All were drafted **before** the Amendment Act No. 22 of 2025 and several rely on provisions that the amendment repealed or replaced, so the DPA will have to re-issue them. On the website these may be described only as "what the DPA proposed in 2024"; never as requirements.

## DRF-01 — DPIA Regulations (1 Oct 2024)

- A DPIA (in the Schedule I form) is required before processing involving (a) systematic and extensive evaluation incl. profiling, (b) systematic monitoring of publicly accessible areas or telecom networks, (c) activities set by future rules. Defines "systematic" and "extensive".
- Content: responsible contact, DPO, overview, purposes/benefits, department, staff/processors/experts consulted; description of data (types, special categories, numbers, volume, sensitivity) and processing (collection, access, sharing, cross-border per s.26, retention, security per s.10); impact on each Part I obligation (ss.5–12) and each Part II right (ss.13–19); risk of harm under any written law; example harms; mitigation; conclusion.
- Schedule II risk matrix 5×5 (probability × impact); score ≥10 triggers a DPIA; a controller may do one anyway.
- Reg 13: electronic copy to the Authority. Reg 14: consult the Authority where risks cannot be mitigated or for national security/public order/public health.
- Gaps: no deadline before processing starts, no periodic review, no submission deadline; Reg 12(1) "may" vs 12(2) mandatory threshold.
- Website relevance: CCTV in shops (24(1)(b) "publicly accessible areas") — a key small-business scenario, but say "the Act requires a DPIA for systematic monitoring; the form and detail are not yet prescribed".

## DRF-02 — Breach Notification Rules (1 Oct 2024)

- Applies to controllers; processor/sub-processor breaches count as the controller's.
- Notify the Authority for any breach unless unlikely to result in risk / likely low risk; notify data subjects where high risk.
- **72 hours** "to the extent feasible" from awareness (or reasonable determination a breach likely occurred); reasons required for delay. Data subjects notified at the same time.
- Content set out in Schedules I (Authority) and II (data subjects), incl. DPO contact, consequences, measures, recommended steps for individuals, certification by DPO/secretary/CEO/head.
- Direct notification methods (email, SMS, calls, messaging apps, webinars, post) or public notice where direct notice is disproportionate; plain language; Authority may order further notices at controller's cost.
- Low-risk presumptions: recoverable from backup, already public, encrypted with uncompromised keys, mitigated by e.g. password resets.
- Record-keeping of all breaches, risk assessments and notices.
- Drafting errors noted (cross-references to rules 4/122/155; "Regulations" vs "Rules"; declaration wording).
- Website relevance: the "72 hours" figure is widely reported as law — it is not. s.23 is in operation from 1 Jan 2027 but the form, manner and period are "as may be determined by rules" which do not exist.

## DRF-03 — Cross-Border Instruments Directive (2 Oct 2024)

- Applies to controllers/processors other than public authorities (public authorities were, under old s.26(1), barred from processing abroad unless categories were classified).
- Instruments: binding corporate rules; agreement with recipient; code of conduct with mandatory monitoring; binding certification scheme (Schedule recognises APEC CBPR, Europrivacy, accredited EU/Swiss/UK GDPR schemes, substantially similar schemes); cross-border processing impact assessment (CBPIA — assess third-country law/enforcement and recipient commitments); board resolution with annual compliance reporting for recipients outside the Act's reach.
- Instruments must impose written, binding, enforceable obligations on the recipient and give data subjects enforceable rights.
- Copies to the Authority on request, but not until three years after s.26 commences (Dir. 12).
- Status after amendment: new s.26(2) requires "such instruments as may be specified by a directive issued by the Authority under paragraph (c) of section 33"; new s.26(3) exceptions mirror old s.26(5); public-authority restriction now limited to categories the Minister prescribes under s.26(5). A re-issued directive is expected (CIR-02 para 4.6 refers to "a draft directive already published").
- Website relevance: cloud/WhatsApp/Microsoft 365/Google Workspace scenario for small businesses — from 1 Jan 2027 s.26 applies, but the instruments are not yet specified; explicit informed consent or contractual necessity (s.26(3)(a),(b)) are the statutory exceptions.

## DRF-04 — DPO Regulations (3 Oct 2024)

- Trigger factors for "scale and magnitude" (monitoring or special-category processing as core activity): ≥25,000 data subjects (current or within 12 months); processing carried out by ≥20 persons; volume; range of data items; geographic extent, frequency/nature/purpose and duration of monitoring. Only the 25,000 figure is numeric.
- Qualifications: degree in law, public/business administration, IT, information security, computer science or related field, plus good understanding of data protection law.
- Definitions: core activities (excludes ancillary/support functions); monitoring (video/voice recording, electronic communications, geo-location, metadata, internet tracking/profiling, connected devices); regular and systematic.
- Not addressed: publication/notification of DPO details (that is in s.20(4) itself), group DPOs, outsourcing, transition.
- Website relevance: lets small businesses see that the proposed threshold is high, but the threshold is **not law**; s.20(1)(b)(iii) (risk of harm) has no numeric limit and now depends on DPA guidelines not yet issued.

## DRF-05 — Rights and Appeals Regulations (5 Oct 2024)

- Documents for representatives: minors (birth certificate / guardianship proof), court-appointed guardians, letters of authorisation, heirs (death certificate + proof of heirship).
- Appeal to the Authority in the Schedule form, by registered post or electronic channel, within **three months** of the controller's decision, with the original request and decision attached; acknowledgement within 7 working days.
- Authority may disallow if outside s.19(1), late, wrong person, or repetitive; not on technical grounds. Decision within **three months**, extendable in 6-week increments with notice. Withdrawal allowed.
- Gaps: no request form to the controller, no identity-verification procedure, no fees; regulation numbering skips (4→6→9→12→16).

## DRF-06 — Inquiry Procedure Regulations (5 Oct 2024)

- Complaint by any aggrieved person in the Schedule form, or own-motion inquiry; Director-General appoints an Inquiry Officer; preliminary rejection if contravention unclear or evidence lacking.
- ≥45 days' notice to parties; representation, written responses, evidence, cross-examination, confidentiality requests; public hearings by default; ex parte if non-appearance; recorded and transcribed.
- Delegated powers: summon, produce documents, inspect/retain records, administer oaths.
- Inquiry concluded within **90 days** (extendable with DG approval); report to DG, to Board within a week; decision communicated within three weeks; directive under s.35(2) or dismissal.
- No compensation mechanism and no publication of decisions (confidential subject to the RTI Act No. 12 of 2016).

## DRF-07 — Fees Regulation (12 Oct 2024)

- Controller may charge up to **Rs 1,000 per request** only where the request is manifestly unreasonable/unfounded/excessive/bad-faith (objective proportionality test; justification to data subject and Authority) or for further copies under s.13.
- Superseded in substance by the amended s.17(6) (free of charge; DPA may set fee criteria by rules). Treat as historical.

## What this means for the website (Phase 2 rule)

Every page that touches breach notification, DPOs, DPIAs, cross-border transfers, appeals, complaints or fees must carry the line: "The Data Protection Authority published draft rules on this in 2024. They are not in force and may change. This page will be updated when final rules are gazetted."

## The situation

You run a grocery, hardware shop, salon or pharmacy and take customers' names and phone numbers — for deliveries, to tell them when an order is ready, for a loyalty scheme, or to send offers.

## What the Act says

| Provision | What it requires here | Status |
|---|---|---|
| s.2 — Application | Your customer list is business processing, not household use; the Act applies. | 2027 |
| s.5 + Sch I — Lawful basis | You need a legal reason for each use: delivering an order is 'necessary for the contract' (Sch I(b)); sending offers needs consent (and, under Part IV, consent is the only basis for marketing). | 2027 (s.27: NO) |
| s.6, s.7 — Purpose and minimisation | Collect the number for a stated purpose and nothing more than you need. | 2027 |
| s.9 — Retention | Do not keep numbers indefinitely once the purpose has ended. | 2027 |
| s.10 — Security | Keep the list reasonably secure (locked phone/PC, not shared logins). | 2027 |
| s.11 + Sch V — Transparency | Tell customers, in writing (a counter card or message), who you are, why you want the number, how long you keep it, and whether you will share it. | 2027 |
| s.27 — Marketing messages | Promotional SMS/WhatsApp only with proper consent, with sender identity and a free opt-out. | NO |
| ss.13–16 — Customer rights | Customers will be able to ask what you hold, correct it, or (in limited cases) have it deleted. | NO |

Status key: **IN** = in force now · **2027** = in force from 1 January 2027 (Gazette 2498/16) · **NO** = not in force, no date set (Gazette 2427/34 repealed the 2025 date). Links go to the section explainers in `07-English-Content/sections/`.

## What this means for you

### Now (before 1 January 2027)
No legal duty under the Act yet, but a good time to separate 'service' numbers from 'marketing' numbers and to start asking for opt-in before sending offers.

### From 1 January 2027
Have a stated purpose, a one-line written notice at the point of collection, a sensible retention period, and basic security. Keep the list to what you need.

### When Parts II, IV and VII commence (no date yet)
Marketing without consent becomes unlawful (s.27); customers gain enforceable rights; the Authority's penalty powers become live.

## Common misunderstandings

- 'I need written consent to take a phone number for a delivery.' — No: contract necessity (Sch I(b)) covers it. Consent is needed for *marketing*.
- 'Small shops are exempt.' — No: only purely personal/household use is exempt (s.2(3)).
- 'I must appoint a DPO.' — Almost certainly not; see s.20 (thresholds not yet set).

## Worked example

Nimal's hardware store takes a number to call when a special-order item arrives (contract necessity). He also wants to text monthly promotions. He adds a tick-box: 'Send me offers by SMS (you can stop any time)'. Only ticked customers get promotions. His counter card says who he is, why he takes the number, that it is kept for one year after the last purchase, and that it is not shared.

## The situation

A Sri Lankan business website uses contact forms, newsletter sign-ups, analytics, cookies, embedded chat and ad pixels.

## What the Act says

| Provision | What it requires here | Status |
|---|---|---|
| s.2(1)(b)(iii)–(iv) | Offering goods/services to, or monitoring, people in Sri Lanka brings foreign-hosted sites in scope too. | 2027 |
| s.56 — Personal data | Online identifiers and location data are personal data. | IN (definition) |
| s.5 | Contact form: contract/pre-contract. Analytics/ads: consent or legitimate interests — but tracking that profiles is monitoring. | 2027 |
| s.11 + Sch V | A privacy notice on the site; mention profiling and automated decisions (item 1(m)). | 2027 |
| s.21, s.26 | Analytics, hosting, chat and email providers are processors, usually abroad. | 2027 |
| s.27 | Newsletters and promotional emails need Schedule III consent. | NO |
| s.14 — Objection | Visitors may object to legitimate-interest tracking. | NO |

Status key: **IN** = in force now · **2027** = in force from 1 January 2027 (Gazette 2498/16) · **NO** = not in force, no date set (Gazette 2427/34 repealed the 2025 date). Links go to the section explainers in `07-English-Content/sections/`.

## What this means for you

### Now (before 1 January 2027)
Write a notice; review third-party scripts.

### From 1 January 2027
Notice live; processor terms; lawful basis per script; data minimisation on forms.

### When Parts II, IV and VII commence (no date yet)
Consent-only marketing emails; objection rights.

## Common misunderstandings

- 'The GDPR cookie banner covers us.' — The PDPA has no cookie-specific rule; what matters is lawful basis, notice, and (for marketing) consent.

## Worked example

An online seller's site keeps the contact form to name, email and message, adds a notice page, and separates the newsletter tick-box from the checkout.

## Good practice (not required by the Act)

These are recommendations from the project, not legal requirements, and are labelled as such on the site.

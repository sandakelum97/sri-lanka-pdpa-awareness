# An online seller collecting delivery addresses

## The situation

A seller on Daraz, Facebook, Instagram or its own site collects names, addresses, phone numbers and payment details, and passes them to couriers and payment gateways.

## What the Act says

| Provision | What it requires here | Status |
|---|---|---|
| s.5 + Sch I(b) | Delivery details are contract necessity. | 2027 |
| s.7, s.9 | Only what delivery needs; delete after order completion and any statutory period. | 2027 |
| s.21 — Processors | Courier and payment gateway are processors — contracts/terms needed. | 2027 |
| s.26 — Cross-border | Marketplaces and gateways hosted abroad. | 2027 |
| s.10 — Security | Order spreadsheets, WhatsApp order chats. | 2027 |
| s.27 | Re-marketing by SMS/WhatsApp needs consent. | NO |

Status key: **IN** = in force now · **2027** = in force from 1 January 2027 (Gazette 2498/16) · **NO** = not in force, no date set (Gazette 2427/34 repealed the 2025 date). Links go to the section explainers in `07-English-Content/sections/`.

## What this means for you

### Now (before 1 January 2027)
Clean order records; check courier terms.

### From 1 January 2027
Notice at checkout; retention; processor terms; security of order records.

### When Parts II, IV and VII commence (no date yet)
Marketing consent; customer rights.

## Common misunderstandings

- 'The marketplace is responsible, not me.' — The seller deciding to fulfil the order is a controller; the marketplace may be a controller or processor depending on the arrangement (joint controllers possible, s.21(2)).

## Worked example

A clothing seller deletes delivery addresses 90 days after delivery, keeps invoices as required by tax law with the address only where it must appear, and uses the courier's standard data terms.

## Good practice (not required by the Act)

These are recommendations from the project, not legal requirements, and are labelled as such on the site.

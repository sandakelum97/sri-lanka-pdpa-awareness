## The situation

A business sends bulk SMS, WhatsApp broadcasts or email campaigns to customers and purchased lists.

## What the Act says

| Provision | What it requires here | Status |
|---|---|---|
| s.27 — Solicited messages | Only with Schedule III consent; identify sender; free opt-out in every message; Authority may prescribe prefixes/codes. | NO |
| s.14(1) | Consent can be withdrawn at any time. | NO |
| s.5 + Sch I | The underlying list must have been lawfully collected (purchased lists usually were not). | 2027 |
| s.6 | Numbers collected for deliveries may not be re-used for marketing without a basis. | 2027 |
| s.26 | WhatsApp Business and foreign SMS gateways are cross-border. | 2027 |

Status key: **IN** = in force now · **2027** = in force from 1 January 2027 (Gazette 2498/16) · **NO** = not in force, no date set (Gazette 2427/34 repealed the 2025 date). Links go to the section explainers in `07-English-Content/sections/`.

## What this means for you

### Now (before 1 January 2027)
Build opt-in lists; add sender identity and opt-out to templates; stop buying lists.

### From 1 January 2027
Lawful collection and purpose limitation apply even though s.27 itself is not yet in force.

### When Parts II, IV and VII commence (no date yet)
Consent-only marketing becomes law; the Authority can direct a stop.

## Common misunderstandings

- 'Part IV isn't in force so anything goes.' — Collection and re-use of numbers are still governed by Part I from 2027, and telecom-regulator rules on bulk SMS apply separately.

## Worked example

A salon migrates to a WhatsApp Business broadcast list that customers join by replying 'YES', with 'Reply STOP to unsubscribe' in every message and the salon's name at the top.

These are recommendations from the project, not legal requirements, and are labelled as such on the site.

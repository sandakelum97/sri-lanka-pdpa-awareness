# Section 3 — The Act prevails over other written law

Part —: Preliminary (ss.1–3) · Status: **In force from 1 January 2027**

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> The Act has effect notwithstanding anything to the contrary in any other written law relating to the protection of personal data. Proviso: a public authority governed by another written law may process in accordance with that law in so far as the protection of personal data is consistent with this Act. In the event of inconsistency, this Act prevails. — s.3

## 2. In plain English

Where another Sri Lankan law says something different about protecting personal data, this Act wins. Government bodies may keep following their own statutes as long as the protection they give is consistent with the Act.

## 4. What it means in practice

Sector laws (banking secrecy, the Electronic Transactions Act, the RTI Act, health and education statutes, the Online Safety Act) continue to operate, but on personal-data protection the PDPA sets the floor. How the Act interacts with specific sector laws is open research (OQ-10) — do not publish sector-specific statements until closed.

## 5. Who is affected

Public authorities; regulated sectors (banks, insurers, telcos, health).

## 6. Everyday example

A bank is bound by banking secrecy rules and by the PDPA. Where both protect the customer, both apply; if the banking rule permitted something the PDPA forbids, the PDPA prevails.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Regulated sectors: map your sector rules against Parts I and III and note any gaps.
- Public authorities: see DPA Circular 01/2026 para 1.6 for the Authority's reading of s.3.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

Not a duty-creating section; it resolves conflicts between laws.

## 9. Current legal status

**In force from 1 January 2027.** Appointed by Gazette Extraordinary No. 2498/16 of 22 July 2026 (Order under s.1(3) as substituted by Act No. 22 of 2025). Until that date there is no legal duty under this section.

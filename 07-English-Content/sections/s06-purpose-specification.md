## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> Every controller shall ensure personal data is processed for "specified", "explicit" and "legitimate" purposes and is not further processed in a way incompatible with those purposes. Further processing for public-interest archiving, scientific or historical research or statistics is not incompatible, subject to s.10. — s.6

## 2. In plain English

Decide and state what you are collecting the information for, and don't later use it for something unrelated without a fresh legal basis.

## 4. What it means in practice

Write the purpose down before you collect; tell people the purpose (Schedule V, s.11); check new uses against the original purpose. 'Compatible' is not defined in the Act — treat any use the person would not reasonably expect as a new purpose.

## 5. Who is affected

Every controller. High-risk areas: loyalty programmes, data shared between group companies, data collected for one service and reused for marketing, analytics or AI training.

## 6. Everyday example

A supermarket loyalty card collects purchase history to award points (purpose stated). Using that history to build profiles sold to an insurer would be a new, incompatible purpose.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Keep a one-page 'purposes register' per data set.
- When a new use is proposed, record whether it is compatible; if not, obtain a new legal basis and update your notice.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

A failure to meet this obligation is a contravention of the Act. The Authority may inquire (s.35, in force) and issue a binding directive requiring the controller to stop, rectify or pay compensation (s.35(2)). Administrative penalties of up to Rs 10 million per non-compliance with a directive (s.38) sit in Part VII, which is **not yet in force**. A data subject's right to complain or appeal under Part II is also not yet in force.

## 9. Current legal status

**In force from 1 January 2027.** Appointed by Gazette Extraordinary No. 2498/16 of 22 July 2026 (Order under s.1(3) as substituted by Act No. 22 of 2025). Until that date there is no legal duty under this section. Note that Part II (rights of individuals) and Part VII (penalties) are **not** in force and have no appointed date, so from 1 January 2027 this duty exists in law but cannot yet be enforced through the Part VII penalty process; the Authority's Part V powers (inquiries and directives under s.35) are already in operation.

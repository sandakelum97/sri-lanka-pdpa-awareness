## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> Personal data shall be "accurate" and "kept up to date", with every reasonable step taken to erase or rectify inaccurate or outdated data without undue delay. — s.8

## 2. In plain English

Keep information correct, and fix mistakes promptly when you find them or are told about them.

## 4. What it means in practice

Have a simple way for customers, students, patients or staff to tell you about changes; correct records when told. (The formal right to request rectification in s.15 is in Part II, which is not yet in force — but the duty to be accurate under s.8 is.)

## 5. Who is affected

Every controller. Critical for banks, hospitals, schools, employers, utilities and anyone making decisions based on records.

## 6. Everyday example

A hospital records the wrong blood group. Section 8 requires reasonable steps to keep records accurate — for example checking identity at admission and correcting the record as soon as the error is found.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Add 'is your information still correct?' prompts at natural touchpoints.
- Log corrections and who made them.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

A failure to meet this obligation is a contravention of the Act. The Authority may inquire (s.35, in force) and issue a binding directive requiring the controller to stop, rectify or pay compensation (s.35(2)). Administrative penalties of up to Rs 10 million per non-compliance with a directive (s.38) sit in Part VII, which is **not yet in force**. A data subject's right to complain or appeal under Part II is also not yet in force.

## 9. Current legal status

**In force from 1 January 2027.** Appointed by Gazette Extraordinary No. 2498/16 of 22 July 2026 (Order under s.1(3) as substituted by Act No. 22 of 2025). Until that date there is no legal duty under this section. Note that Part II (rights of individuals) and Part VII (penalties) are **not** in force and have no appointed date, so from 1 January 2027 this duty exists in law but cannot yet be enforced through the Part VII penalty process; the Authority's Part V powers (inquiries and directives under s.35) are already in operation.

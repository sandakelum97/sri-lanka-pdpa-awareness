# Section 9 — Retention limitation

Part I: Processing of Personal Data (ss.4–12) · Status: **In force from 1 January 2027** (Gazette 2498/16)

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> Personal data shall be kept in a form permitting identification only for as long as necessary for the purposes of processing; longer storage is allowed for public-interest archiving, scientific/historical research or statistics, subject to s.10. — s.9

## 2. In plain English

Don't keep people's information forever. Keep it as long as you need it for the purpose (or as long as another law requires you to), then delete it or anonymise it.

## 4. What it means in practice

Set a retention period for each data set. Other laws may *require* retention (tax, employment, company records) — those periods are the minimum; the Act asks you not to keep identifiable data beyond what is necessary. Retention periods must also be told to people (Schedule V item 1(i)). Research into the specific periods required by Sri Lankan tax, labour and company law is open (OQ-11) — do not quote periods until it is closed.

## 5. Who is affected

Every controller. Especially CCTV operators (recordings), recruiters (CVs of unsuccessful applicants), clinics, schools and anyone with old paper files.

## 6. Everyday example

A tuition class still holds the records of students who left five years ago. Unless another law requires it, s.9 expects those records to be deleted or anonymised once they are no longer needed.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Write a retention schedule (data set → period → reason → disposal method).
- Dispose securely: shred paper; securely wipe devices before resale.
- For CCTV, set an automatic overwrite period.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

A failure to meet this obligation is a contravention of the Act. The Authority may inquire (s.35, in force) and issue a binding directive requiring the controller to stop, rectify or pay compensation (s.35(2)). Administrative penalties of up to Rs 10 million per non-compliance with a directive (s.38) sit in Part VII, which is **not yet in force**. A data subject's right to complain or appeal under Part II is also not yet in force.

## 9. Current legal status

**In force from 1 January 2027.** Appointed by Gazette Extraordinary No. 2498/16 of 22 July 2026 (Order under s.1(3) as substituted by Act No. 22 of 2025). Until that date there is no legal duty under this section. Note that Part II (rights of individuals) and Part VII (penalties) are **not** in force and have no appointed date, so from 1 January 2027 this duty exists in law but cannot yet be enforced through the Part VII penalty process; the Authority's Part V powers (inquiries and directives under s.35) are already in operation.

# Section 10 — Integrity and confidentiality (security)

Part I: Processing of Personal Data (ss.4–12) · Status: **In force from 1 January 2027** (Gazette 2498/16)

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> Every controller shall ensure integrity and confidentiality of personal data by "appropriate technical and organizational measures including encryption, pseudonymisation, anonymisation or access controls or such other measures as may be prescribed", so as to prevent unauthorised or unlawful processing, and loss, destruction or damage. — s.10

## 2. In plain English

Protect the information you hold so it is not leaked, lost, stolen or tampered with. The law names encryption, pseudonymisation, anonymisation and access controls as examples; 'appropriate' means proportionate to your size and the sensitivity of the data.

## 4. What it means in practice

This is a legal duty, but the Act does not prescribe a specific standard (no regulations under 'as may be prescribed' exist). Everything beyond the four named examples is good practice, not statute. For a small business, 'appropriate' will usually mean: locked cabinets, passwords and screen locks, software updates, not sharing logins, backups, and care with WhatsApp and personal phones. For a hospital or bank, considerably more.

## 5. Who is affected

Every controller (and processors, through s.22(1)(b) and their contract).

## 6. Everyday example

A shop keeps its customer list in an Excel file on an unlocked laptop that staff share. If the laptop is stolen, the shop has failed to take appropriate measures. A password-protected device with the file encrypted and backed up would be a proportionate measure.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Legal duty (s.10): choose and document measures proportionate to your data.
- Good practice (not statute): see the Small Business Health Check — device locks, updates, MFA on email, backups, least-privilege access, staff briefing, secure disposal.
- Keep the distinction visible on every page: *required by s.10* vs *recommended*.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

A failure to meet this obligation is a contravention of the Act. The Authority may inquire (s.35, in force) and issue a binding directive requiring the controller to stop, rectify or pay compensation (s.35(2)). Administrative penalties of up to Rs 10 million per non-compliance with a directive (s.38) sit in Part VII, which is **not yet in force**. A data subject's right to complain or appeal under Part II is also not yet in force.

## 9. Current legal status

**In force from 1 January 2027.** Appointed by Gazette Extraordinary No. 2498/16 of 22 July 2026 (Order under s.1(3) as substituted by Act No. 22 of 2025). Until that date there is no legal duty under this section. Note that Part II (rights of individuals) and Part VII (penalties) are **not** in force and have no appointed date, so from 1 January 2027 this duty exists in law but cannot yet be enforced through the Part VII penalty process; the Authority's Part V powers (inquiries and directives under s.35) are already in operation.

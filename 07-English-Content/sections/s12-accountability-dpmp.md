## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> Every controller must implement internal controls and procedures — the "Data Protection Management Programme" — that: (a) establish and maintain catalogued records demonstrating how ss.5–11 are complied with; (b) are designed on the basis of the "structure, scale, volume and sensitivity" of the controller's processing; (c) provide safeguards based on DPIAs under s.24; (d) are integrated into the controller's governance; (e) establish internal oversight; (f) include a mechanism to receive complaints, conduct inquiries and identify breaches; (g) are updated on periodic monitoring; (h) facilitate the exercise of data-subject rights under ss.13–16 and 18. — s.12(1)

The Amendment Act repealed s.12(2) and inserted s.51A, under which the Authority may issue guidelines, including on the DPMP.

## 2. In plain English

You have to be able to *show* that you comply, not just comply. For a small business this is a short set of written records and a routine; for a large organisation it is a programme with policies, training, oversight and audits. The Act expressly says the programme should be scaled to your size and the sensitivity of your data.

## 4. What it means in practice

Minimum content for any controller: a record of what data you hold and why (your s.5/s.6 register), your privacy notice, your retention schedule, your security measures, a way for people to complain, and a way to spot and handle breaches. No DPMP guidelines have been issued by the Authority yet (s.51A power exists; nothing published as at 31 Oct 2026). The DPA's Circular 01/2026 Annexure 1 lists six 'initial steps' for public bodies which work equally as a starting point for anyone: awareness, governance structure, personal data audit, gap assessment, policy implementation, training.

## 5. Who is affected

Every controller; scaled by s.12(1)(b).

## 6. Everyday example

A 10-person export company keeps a two-page document: what personal data it holds (customers, staff, suppliers), the legal basis, where it is stored, who can access it, how long it is kept, and who to call if something goes wrong. That is a proportionate DPMP. A bank's DPMP will be a full policy framework with a DPO, DPIAs and audits.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Start with the six steps in Circular 01/2026 Annexure 1.
- Keep the records simple but written; the Authority may inspect them (s.32(b), s.32(h)).
- Review annually.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

Failure to maintain a DPMP is a contravention; in addition, s.39(c) makes the *effectiveness* of your DPMP a factor in setting any penalty (Part VII, not yet in force).

## 9. Current legal status

**In force from 1 January 2027.** Appointed by Gazette Extraordinary No. 2498/16 of 22 July 2026 (Order under s.1(3) as substituted by Act No. 22 of 2025). Until that date there is no legal duty under this section. Note that Part II (rights of individuals) and Part VII (penalties) are **not** in force and have no appointed date, so from 1 January 2027 this duty exists in law but cannot yet be enforced through the Part VII penalty process; the Authority's Part V powers (inquiries and directives under s.35) are already in operation.

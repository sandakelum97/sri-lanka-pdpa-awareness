# Section 13 — Right of access

Part II: Rights of Data Subjects (ss.13–19) · Status: **NOT IN FORCE — no date set**

> ⚠️ **This provision is not yet in force.** No start date has been set. Read section 9 below before relying on anything here.

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> Every data subject has the right, on a written request to the controller, to access their personal data, to confirmation of whether it has been processed, and to the Schedule V information; the controller shall provide it subject to s.17. — s.13

## 2. In plain English

Once in force, you will be able to write to any business or body and ask: do you hold information about me, what is it, why, who do you share it with, how long do you keep it.

## 4. What it means in practice

The request must be in writing (which includes electronic). The controller's response procedure and permitted refusals are in s.17 (as amended): one month, extendable to three, free of charge, with reasons for any refusal and notice of the right to appeal. Identity verification may be requested (s.17(4)). Nothing stops a controller from answering such requests voluntarily before commencement, and the DPA's own privacy notice shows it intends to.

## 5. Who is affected

Every individual (including, for deceased persons, their heirs for ten years — s.17(5)(d)); parents/guardians for minors and the incapacitated; every controller.

## 6. Everyday example

A customer writes to her bank asking what personal data it holds about her and who it is shared with. When s.13 is in force the bank must answer within one month with the Schedule V particulars, unless a s.17(2) ground for refusal applies.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Individuals: keep a copy of any request you send and the date.
- Controllers: build the response process now (it is also required by s.12(1)(h) for your DPMP).

## 8. Consequences of not complying

None at present — the section is not in force. Once commenced, a refusal can be appealed to the Authority under s.19 (also Part II), and non-compliance with a resulting Authority directive would attract Part VII penalties if Part VII is also in force.

## 9. Current legal status

**NOT IN FORCE — no commencement date has been set.** This section was scheduled to commence on 18 March 2025 by Gazette 2366/08, but that date was repealed by Gazette 2427/34 on 14 March 2025 and no new Order has been made. The Data Protection Authority's own Data Protection Statement (updated 20 August 2026) confirms that Part II rights and Part VII penalties are not yet in force and "cannot be exercised right now". Everything on this page describes what the law *will* say once the Minister appoints a date by Gazette. It creates no present entitlement or liability. This page will be updated the day a commencement Order is published.

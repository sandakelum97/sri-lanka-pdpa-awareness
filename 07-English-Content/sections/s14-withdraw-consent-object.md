# Section 14 — Withdrawing consent; objecting to processing

Part II: Rights of Data Subjects (ss.13–19) · Status: **NOT IN FORCE — no date set**

> ⚠️ **This provision is not yet in force.** No start date has been set. Read section 9 below before relying on anything here.

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> (1) Every data subject has the right to withdraw consent at any time by written request where processing is based on consent (Sch I(a) or Sch II(a)); withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of earlier processing. (2) Every data subject has the right to request in writing that the controller refrain from further processing where processing is based on public interest/official functions (Sch I(e)), legitimate interests (Sch I(f)) or Sch II(f). — s.14

## 2. In plain English

If a business is using your information because you agreed, you can take that agreement back at any time. If it is using your information for its own 'legitimate interests' or a public-interest task, you can object and ask it to stop.

## 4. What it means in practice

Withdrawal is not retrospective. An objection under s.14(2) is a request; the controller may refuse on s.17(2) grounds and must give reasons. Note the Act gives **no** objection right where processing rests on contract or legal obligation.

## 5. Who is affected

Individuals; every controller relying on consent, legitimate interests or public-interest grounds.

## 6. Everyday example

A customer who agreed to receive promotional emails from an online store withdraws consent. The store must stop the consent-based processing; it may still process what is needed to complete orders (Sch I(b)).

## 7. What to consider doing

- Controllers: make withdrawal as easy as giving consent (Sch III(d) already requires telling people they can withdraw).

## 8. Consequences of not complying

None at present — the section is not in force. Once commenced, a refusal can be appealed to the Authority under s.19 (also Part II), and non-compliance with a resulting Authority directive would attract Part VII penalties if Part VII is also in force.

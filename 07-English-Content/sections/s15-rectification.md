> ⚠️ **This provision is not yet in force.** No start date has been set. Read section 9 below before relying on anything here.

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> Every data subject has the right to request in writing that a controller rectify or complete inaccurate or incomplete personal data; the controller shall do so without undue delay, subject to s.17. Provisos: no obligation to collect additional data not needed for the purpose; where data must be retained for evidentiary purposes under law or court order, the controller refrains from further processing instead of rectifying. — s.15

## 2. In plain English

You will be able to require a business to correct wrong information about you or fill in what is missing.

## 4. What it means in practice

The duty to keep data accurate (s.8) is in force from 1 January 2027 regardless; s.15 adds the individual's enforceable request. Evidentiary records (e.g. a disputed transaction log) are not altered but frozen.

## 5. Who is affected

Individuals; every controller.

## 6. Everyday example

A patient notices her hospital record shows the wrong date of birth. Under s.15 she can require correction; the hospital must correct it without undue delay.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Controllers: a correction log with who/when/what.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

None at present — the section is not in force. Once commenced, a refusal can be appealed to the Authority under s.19 (also Part II), and non-compliance with a resulting Authority directive would attract Part VII penalties if Part VII is also in force.

## 9. Current legal status

**NOT IN FORCE — no commencement date has been set.** This section was scheduled to commence on 18 March 2025 by Gazette 2366/08, but that date was repealed by Gazette 2427/34 on 14 March 2025 and no new Order has been made. The Data Protection Authority's own Data Protection Statement (updated 20 August 2026) confirms that Part II rights and Part VII penalties are not yet in force and "cannot be exercised right now".

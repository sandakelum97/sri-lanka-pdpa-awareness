# Section 16 — Right to erasure

Part II: Rights of Data Subjects (ss.13–19) · Status: **NOT IN FORCE — no date set**

> ⚠️ **This provision is not yet in force.** No start date has been set. Read section 9 below before relying on anything here.

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> Every data subject has the right to request in writing that the controller erase their personal data where (a) processing contravened ss.5–11; (b) the data subject withdraws the consent on which processing is based; or (c) erasure is required by written law or a court order. — s.16

## 2. In plain English

In three situations you will be able to require deletion: the business broke the processing rules; you withdrew consent that was the only basis; or a law or court says delete. This is **narrower** than the European 'right to be forgotten' — there is no general 'I no longer want you to have it' ground.

## 4. What it means in practice

Controllers must map each erasure request to one of the three grounds; otherwise refusal with reasons under s.17(2). Retention required by other written law (s.17(2)(h)) will often defeat erasure of accounting, employment or medical records.

## 5. Who is affected

Individuals; every controller.

## 6. Everyday example

A former gym member asks the gym to delete her file. If the gym held it on consent that she now withdraws, ground (b) applies. The gym may still retain invoices it must keep under tax law.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Controllers: a deletion procedure that checks the three grounds and your retention schedule (s.9).

## 8. Consequences of not complying

None at present — the section is not in force. Once commenced, a refusal can be appealed to the Authority under s.19 (also Part II), and non-compliance with a resulting Authority directive would attract Part VII penalties if Part VII is also in force.

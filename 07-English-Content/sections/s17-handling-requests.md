> ⚠️ **This provision is not yet in force.** No start date has been set. Read section 9 below before relying on anything here.

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> (1) On a written request under ss.13, 14, 15, 16 or 18, the controller shall inform the data subject in writing, "without undue delay and within one month", whether the request was granted or refused with reasons (unless disclosure is prohibited by written law). (1A) The period may be extended by a further two months, not exceeding three months in total, for reasons assigned. (1B) The data subject must be told of the extension before the first month expires. (1C) The controller shall inform the data subject of the right of appeal to the Authority. (2) Refusal is permitted having regard to: national security; public order; inquiries/investigations under written law; prevention or prosecution of crime; rights and freedoms of others; technical/operational feasibility; inability to establish identity; a requirement to process under written law. (3) Reasons for refusal must be recorded and given to the Authority on request. (4) Identity may be verified. (5) Rights may be exercised by parents/guardians of minors, court-appointed guardians, authorised persons, or an heir within ten years of death, "in the manner prescribed by regulations". (6) Requests are free of charge; the Authority may by rules set criteria for when a fee may be charged. — s.17 as amended

## 2. In plain English

When you make a request, the business must answer within one month (or up to three if it tells you why), in writing, for free, saying yes or no with reasons, and telling you that you can appeal to the Data Protection Authority if refused.

## 4. What it means in practice

The 2022 Act's 21-working-day deadline and fee regime were replaced in 2025. The regulations on how representatives prove their standing (s.17(5)) and any fee rules (s.17(6)) do not yet exist; the 2024 drafts (DRF-05, DRF-07) are not law and the fees draft rests on a repealed provision.

## 5. Who is affected

Individuals and their representatives; every controller.

## 6. Everyday example

A parent writes to a tuition centre asking to see what it holds about her 14-year-old. The centre may ask for proof she is the parent (s.17(4)–(5)), must reply within one month, free, and must mention the right of appeal.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Controllers: a request register with received date, one-month and three-month clocks, decision, reasons, and whether the appeal right was stated.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

None at present — the section is not in force. Once commenced, a refusal can be appealed to the Authority under s.19 (also Part II), and non-compliance with a resulting Authority directive would attract Part VII penalties if Part VII is also in force.

## 9. Current legal status

**NOT IN FORCE — no commencement date has been set.** This section was scheduled to commence on 18 March 2025 by Gazette 2366/08, but that date was repealed by Gazette 2427/34 on 14 March 2025 and no new Order has been made. The Data Protection Authority's own Data Protection Statement (updated 20 August 2026) confirms that Part II rights and Part VII penalties are not yet in force and "cannot be exercised right now". Everything on this page describes what the law *will* say once the Minister appoints a date by Gazette. It creates no present entitlement or liability.

> ⚠️ **This provision is not yet in force.** No start date has been set. Read section 9 below before relying on anything here.

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> Subject to s.19, every data subject has the right to request a controller to review a decision based solely on automated processing which has created, or is likely to create, "an irreversible and continuous impact on the rights and freedoms of the data subject under the Constitution or any written law". — s.18(1) as amended. (2) Does not apply where the decision is authorised by written law, authorised in a manner determined by the Authority, based on consent, or necessary for a contract — but the controller must comply with safeguards specified by Authority rules; the contract exception does not apply to special categories.

## 2. In plain English

If a computer alone made a decision about you that has a lasting, serious effect — say a credit refusal or an automated job-screening rejection — you will be able to ask a human to review it. The 2025 amendment made clear this covers impacts on constitutional rights such as equality and non-discrimination.

## 4. What it means in practice

The threshold ('irreversible and continuous impact') is high and untested. Authority rules on safeguards (s.18(2)) do not exist. This is the Act's main hook for AI-driven decisions.

## 5. Who is affected

Lenders, insurers, recruiters, platforms, public bodies using automated eligibility decisions; affected individuals.

## 6. Everyday example

An applicant is rejected for a loan by an automated scoring model with no human involvement. Under s.18 she may request review; the bank may rely on the contract exception but must then apply the Authority's safeguard rules (once made) and cannot do so for special-category data.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Organisations: inventory decisions made solely by automation; design a human-review route.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

None at present — the section is not in force. Once commenced, a refusal can be appealed to the Authority under s.19 (also Part II), and non-compliance with a resulting Authority directive would attract Part VII penalties if Part VII is also in force.

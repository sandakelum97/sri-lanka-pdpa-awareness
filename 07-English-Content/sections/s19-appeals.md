> ⚠️ **This provision is not yet in force.** No start date has been set. Read section 9 below before relying on anything here.

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> (1) Where a controller has refused access (s.13), refused withdrawal of consent or to refrain from processing (s.14), refused rectification (s.15), refused erasure (s.16), refused a request under s.17(2), or refused to review an automated decision (s.18(1)), the data subject may appeal to the Authority in the prescribed form, manner and time. (2) The Authority may determine whether each refusal was lawful. (3) After investigation the Authority shall determine within the prescribed period whether the appeal is allowed or disallowed, with reasons, and inform both parties. (4) If allowed, the controller must give effect to the decision within the time the Authority sets. (5) Either party may appeal to the Court of Appeal within thirty days. — s.19 as amended

## 2. In plain English

If a business refuses your request, you will be able to appeal to the Data Protection Authority, which can order the business to comply. Either side can take the Authority's decision to the Court of Appeal within 30 days.

## 4. What it means in practice

Form, manner, time limits and the Authority's decision period are all 'as may be prescribed' — no regulations exist (the 2024 draft, DRF-05, proposed three months to appeal and three months to decide; not law). This is the route by which Part VII penalties eventually attach: an Authority directive under s.35 that is not complied with triggers s.38.

## 5. Who is affected

Individuals; controllers.

## 6. Everyday example

A customer's access request is refused on 'technical feasibility' grounds. She appeals to the Authority, which finds the refusal unlawful and directs disclosure within 14 days.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Controllers: keep the s.17(3) refusal record — it is what the Authority will ask for.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

None at present — the section is not in force. Once commenced, failure to give effect to an Authority decision would ground a s.35 directive and, when Part VII is in force, a s.38 penalty.

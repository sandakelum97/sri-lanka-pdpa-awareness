# Section 20 — Data Protection Officer

Part III: Controllers and Processors (ss.20–26) · Status: **In force from 1 January 2027** (Gazette 2498/16)

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> Every controller and processor shall designate or appoint a DPO where: (a) the processing is carried out by "a Ministry or government department" (except the judiciary acting judicially) [as amended — public corporations removed]; or (b) its core activities consist of (i) operations requiring regular and systematic monitoring of data subjects "on a scale and magnitude as may be prescribed"; (ii) processing of special categories "on a scale and magnitude as may be prescribed"; or (iii) categories of processing resulting in a risk of harm to data subjects' rights "as may be determined by the Authority by way of guidelines" [as amended]. — s.20(1)

s.20(2): qualifications as may be prescribed. s.20(3): one DPO for a group or for several public authorities. s.20(4): publish the DPO's contact details and communicate them to the Authority. s.20(5) (as amended): advise on obligations, advise on how to comply, build staff capacity, advise on DPIAs, co-operate with the Authority. s.56 (as amended): a DPO may be an external third party.

## 2. In plain English

Some organisations must appoint a Data Protection Officer — a named person responsible for compliance. Government ministries and departments always must. Private organisations must only if they do large-scale monitoring or large-scale handling of sensitive data (the thresholds are not yet set), or fall into risk categories the Authority will define in guidelines (not yet issued). Since the 2025 amendment, the DPO can be an outsourced consultant.

## 4. What it means in practice

As at 31 Oct 2026 no regulations prescribe the 'scale and magnitude' thresholds and no guidelines define the risk categories, so **no private-sector controller can yet be certain it is caught by s.20(1)(b)**. The 2024 draft regulation proposed a 25,000-data-subject / 20-staff indicator (DRF-04) — a draft only. Public corporations were removed from the mandatory list by the amendment, but the DPA says they may still be caught under s.20(1)(b) (Circular 01/2026 para 3.2).

## 5. Who is affected

Ministries and government departments (mandatory). Hospitals, insurers, telcos, banks, large retailers, schools with large biometric/CCTV systems, data analytics firms — likely, once thresholds exist. Small businesses — unlikely, but not excluded until thresholds are published.

## 6. Everyday example

A private hospital processes health data for tens of thousands of patients as its core activity. It should expect to need a DPO once thresholds are prescribed and should plan for one now. A hair salon with 300 customers' phone numbers almost certainly will not.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Mandatory now for Ministries/Departments: appoint, publish contact details, notify the Authority.
- Others: assess your core activities against s.20(1)(b); if plausibly caught, designate someone now and watch for the regulations/guidelines.
- Do not state on any public material that a specific threshold applies.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

A failure to meet this obligation is a contravention of the Act. The Authority may inquire (s.35, in force) and issue a binding directive requiring the controller to stop, rectify or pay compensation (s.35(2)). Administrative penalties of up to Rs 10 million per non-compliance with a directive (s.38) sit in Part VII, which is **not yet in force**. A data subject's right to complain or appeal under Part II is also not yet in force.

## 9. Current legal status

**In force from 1 January 2027.** Appointed by Gazette Extraordinary No. 2498/16 of 22 July 2026 (Order under s.1(3) as substituted by Act No. 22 of 2025). Until that date there is no legal duty under this section. Note that Part II (rights of individuals) and Part VII (penalties) are **not** in force and have no appointed date, so from 1 January 2027 this duty exists in law but cannot yet be enforced through the Part VII penalty process; the Authority's Part V powers (inquiries and directives under s.35) are already in operation.

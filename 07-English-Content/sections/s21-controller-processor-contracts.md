## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> Where a processor processes on a controller's behalf, the controller shall (a) use only processors who ensure appropriate technical and organisational measures and protect data-subject rights; and (b) ensure the processor is bound by a contract or written law setting out the subject-matter and duration, nature and purpose, type of personal data and categories of data subjects, and the obligations of the controller. — s.21(1). Two or more controllers jointly determining purposes and means are "joint controllers", jointly responsible. — s.21(2)

## 2. In plain English

If another company handles personal data for you — a payroll bureau, a courier, a cloud provider, a marketing agency, an IT support firm — you must choose one that keeps data safe and you must have a written contract covering what they do with the data.

## 4. What it means in practice

Review every vendor that touches personal data. Standard cloud and SaaS terms often already contain data-processing clauses; check they cover the s.21(1)(b) items. Where you share control with another business (a franchise, a joint promotion), you are joint controllers and both are responsible.

## 5. Who is affected

Any controller that outsources; any two businesses that share a customer database.

## 6. Everyday example

An online seller uses a courier to deliver orders and a Gmail/Google Workspace account to run the business. The courier and Google are processors. The seller needs a contract or terms with each covering the s.21(1)(b) elements, and should pick vendors with adequate security.

## 7. What to consider doing

- List your processors.
- Check each contract/terms for the five s.21(1)(b) elements; add a data-processing clause where missing.
- Keep evidence of why you consider each processor 'appropriate'.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

A failure to meet this obligation is a contravention of the Act. The Authority may inquire (s.35, in force) and issue a binding directive requiring the controller to stop, rectify or pay compensation (s.35(2)). Administrative penalties of up to Rs 10 million per non-compliance with a directive (s.38) sit in Part VII, which is **not yet in force**. A data subject's right to complain or appeal under Part II is also not yet in force.

## 9. Current legal status

**In force from 1 January 2027.** Appointed by Gazette Extraordinary No. 2498/16 of 22 July 2026 (Order under s.1(3) as substituted by Act No. 22 of 2025). Until that date there is no legal duty under this section. Note that Part II (rights of individuals) and Part VII (penalties) are **not** in force and have no appointed date, so from 1 January 2027 this duty exists in law but cannot yet be enforced through the Part VII penalty process; the Authority's Part V powers (inquiries and directives under s.35) are already in operation.

# Section 22 — Duties of processors and sub-processors

Part III: Controllers and Processors (ss.20–26) · Status: **In force from 1 January 2027** (Gazette 2498/16)

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> A processor shall: (a) process only on the controller's written instructions; (b) bind its personnel to confidentiality; (c) facilitate compliance audits and inspections on the controller's written request; (d) on written instruction, erase or return all personal data at the end of the service. A processor that ignores instructions or decides purposes and means itself is deemed a controller for that processing (s.22(2)). The same duties apply to sub-processors (s.22(3)); the processor is liable to the controller for its sub-processors (s.22(4)). 'Personnel' includes employees, consultants, agents, affiliates and contractors (s.22(5)). — s.22

## 2. In plain English

If you handle data for other businesses, you may only do what they tell you in writing, keep it confidential, let them audit you, and hand back or delete the data at the end. If you start using the data for your own purposes, you become a controller with all of a controller's duties.

## 4. What it means in practice

Processors in Sri Lanka — BPO/KPO firms, IT service providers, cloud hosts, payroll and HR outsourcers, marketing agencies, printers, document-storage firms — will need written instructions, NDAs/confidentiality clauses for all personnel, an audit-cooperation clause, and an exit/deletion process. Sub-contracting flows the duties down.

## 5. Who is affected

Every processor and sub-processor; and controllers, because s.22 sets what you should demand in contracts.

## 6. Everyday example

A BPO handling a telecom company's customer-care data must act only on the telco's written instructions. If it uses the customer list to market its own services, it becomes a controller for that use and must meet ss.4–12 itself.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Processors: template clauses for instructions, confidentiality, audit and exit; sub-processor register.
- Controllers: require these clauses (see s.21).

## 8. Consequences of not complying

A failure to meet this obligation is a contravention of the Act. The Authority may inquire (s.35, in force) and issue a binding directive requiring the controller to stop, rectify or pay compensation (s.35(2)). Administrative penalties of up to Rs 10 million per non-compliance with a directive (s.38) sit in Part VII, which is **not yet in force**. A data subject's right to complain or appeal under Part II is also not yet in force.

## 9. Current legal status

**In force from 1 January 2027.** Appointed by Gazette Extraordinary No. 2498/16 of 22 July 2026 (Order under s.1(3) as substituted by Act No. 22 of 2025). Until that date there is no legal duty under this section. Note that Part II (rights of individuals) and Part VII (penalties) are **not** in force and have no appointed date, so from 1 January 2027 this duty exists in law but cannot yet be enforced through the Part VII penalty process; the Authority's Part V powers (inquiries and directives under s.35) are already in operation.

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> In the event of a personal data breach, a controller shall notify the Authority "in such form, manner and within such period of time as may be determined by rules made under this Act". The Authority shall provide by rules for the circumstances in which the Authority and affected data subjects must be notified and the form and content of notifications. — s.23. A 'personal data breach' is any act or omission resulting in accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data (s.56).

## 2. In plain English

If customer, patient, student or staff information is leaked, lost, hacked or wrongly disclosed, the law requires you to report it to the Data Protection Authority. **The deadline, the form, and when you must also tell the affected people are all left to rules that have not yet been made.**

## 4. What it means in practice

As at 31 Oct 2026 the only text on timing is a 2024 *draft* rule proposing 72 hours to the Authority and simultaneous notice to affected people where the risk is high (DRF-02). It is not law. Until rules are made, the practical position from 1 January 2027 is: the duty to notify exists, the mechanics do not; the DPA's privacy notice (notice.php) describes an interim breach-reporting process it already accepts (OQ-17). Organisations should prepare an internal breach-response procedure now (that is also part of the DPMP under s.12(1)(f)).

## 5. Who is affected

Every controller. Processors must help the controller (through the contract — see s.21/s.22).

## 6. Everyday example

A tuition centre's laptop containing student and parent details is stolen. That is a personal data breach (loss and possible unauthorised access). From 1 January 2027 the centre must notify the Authority in the prescribed manner — once prescribed — and should in any event record the incident, change passwords, and consider telling the parents.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Write a one-page breach procedure: who to tell internally, what to record, how to contain, when to notify the Authority and affected people.
- Keep a breach log.
- Do **not** publish '72 hours' as a legal deadline.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

Failure to notify will be a contravention once rules exist. s.39(f) makes self-reporting a mitigating factor in any penalty (Part VII, not yet in force).

## 9. Current legal status

**In force from 1 January 2027.** Appointed by Gazette Extraordinary No. 2498/16 of 22 July 2026 (Order under s.1(3) as substituted by Act No. 22 of 2025). Until that date there is no legal duty under this section. Note that Part II (rights of individuals) and Part VII (penalties) are **not** in force and have no appointed date, so from 1 January 2027 this duty exists in law but cannot yet be enforced through the Part VII penalty process; the Authority's Part V powers (inquiries and directives under s.35) are already in operation.

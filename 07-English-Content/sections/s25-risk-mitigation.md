## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> Where a DPIA indicates that processing is likely to result in a risk of harm to data subjects' rights "under this Act", the controller shall take measures to mitigate the risk before processing. — s.25(1) (as amended). The prior-consultation mechanism (old s.25(2)–(6)) has been repealed. Surviving s.25(7): where s.24(1) processing is carried out in relation to national security, public order and public health, the controller shall consult the Authority.

## 2. In plain English

If your impact assessment shows a real risk to people, you must reduce that risk before you start. The 2022 Act's system of consulting the Authority when you couldn't reduce the risk was removed in 2025; consultation is now mandatory only for national-security, public-order and public-health processing.

## 4. What it means in practice

Mitigation is the controller's own responsibility. Public bodies and contractors running s.24(1) systems for security, policing or public-health purposes must consult the Authority.

## 5. Who is affected

Any controller that must do a DPIA; especially public authorities and their contractors.

## 6. Everyday example

A health ministry contractor plans a disease-surveillance platform combining hospital records. This is s.24(1)(a) processing for public health, so under s.25(7) the Authority must be consulted; identified risks must be mitigated before go-live.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Build a 'risk → measure → owner → date' table into every DPIA.
- Public-health/security/public-order projects: plan the s.25(7) consultation into the timeline.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

A failure to meet this obligation is a contravention of the Act. The Authority may inquire (s.35, in force) and issue a binding directive requiring the controller to stop, rectify or pay compensation (s.35(2)). Administrative penalties of up to Rs 10 million per non-compliance with a directive (s.38) sit in Part VII, which is **not yet in force**. A data subject's right to complain or appeal under Part II is also not yet in force.

## 9. Current legal status

**In force from 1 January 2027.** Appointed by Gazette Extraordinary No. 2498/16 of 22 July 2026 (Order under s.1(3) as substituted by Act No. 22 of 2025). Until that date there is no legal duty under this section. Note that Part II (rights of individuals) and Part VII (penalties) are **not** in force and have no appointed date, so from 1 January 2027 this duty exists in law but cannot yet be enforced through the Part VII penalty process; the Authority's Part V powers (inquiries and directives under s.35) are already in operation.

# Section 27 — Marketing (solicited) messages

Part IV: Use of Personal Data to Disseminate Solicited Messages (s.27) · Status: **NOT IN FORCE — no date set**

> ⚠️ **This provision is not yet in force.** No start date has been set. Read section 9 below before relying on anything here.

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> (1) A controller may use postal, telecommunication, electronic or similar means to send messages only if the data subject has consented to receive them ('solicited messages'). (2) Consent must satisfy Schedule III. (3) When collecting contact details and in every message, the controller must tell the person how to opt out free of charge. (4) Messages must identify the nature of the message and the identity of the controller or the third party on whose behalf it is sent. (5) The Authority may, with the relevant regulator, prescribe codes or prefixes to identify categories of message. (6) 'Message' means any written, electronic, oral, pictorial or video message intended to promote goods, services, or any person or organisation, by post, electronic or telecommunication means including automated calling systems — other than internet advertisements a data subject has consented to in exchange for a free service. — s.27

## 2. In plain English

Once in force, promotional SMS, calls, emails, WhatsApp broadcasts and letters may be sent only to people who have properly agreed to receive them, every message must say who it is from and how to stop them, and opting out must be free. Legitimate interests will not be an alternative — consent is the only basis.

## 4. What it means in practice

Part IV is the only Part with no commencement history at all. Its impact on Sri Lankan retail, banking, telecom and political campaigning will be large; businesses should build opt-in lists now. Telecom-regulator rules on bulk SMS apply independently (out of scope).

## 5. Who is affected

Every business that sends promotional messages; marketing agencies; individuals receiving them.

## 6. Everyday example

A clothing shop sends sale alerts by SMS to every number it has ever collected at the till. Under s.27 (when in force) it may message only customers who gave Schedule III consent, must identify itself in each SMS, and must offer a free opt-out.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Businesses: separate 'service' messages (order ready, appointment reminder) from 'promotional' ones; collect explicit opt-in for the latter; add sender identity and opt-out to templates.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

None at present — the section is not in force. Once commenced, sending messages without consent contravenes s.27; the Authority may direct the controller to stop (s.35) and, when Part VII is in force, penalise non-compliance with such a directive.

## 9. Current legal status

**NOT IN FORCE — no commencement date has been set.** Part IV was never included in any commencement Order; the original 24–48-month window in s.1(4) was repealed by Act No. 22 of 2025, so Part IV now commences only when the Minister appoints a date by Gazette. Everything on this page describes what the law *will* say. It creates no present duty. Note that other laws and regulator rules on SMS/telemarketing (e.g. TRCSL requirements) may apply independently — outside the scope of this page.

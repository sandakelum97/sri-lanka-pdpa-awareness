# Who is the Data Protection Authority of Sri Lanka?

Part V (ss.28–35) · Status: **In force since 17 July 2023** (Gazette 2341/59); Director-General and staff (Part VI), Fund (Part VIII) and the rule- and regulation-making powers (Part IX) since 1 December 2023 (Gazette 2366/08).

## What it is
The Data Protection Authority of Sri Lanka ("the Authority" or "DPA") is the independent regulator created by s.28 of the Act. It is a body corporate run by a Board of five to seven members appointed by the President (s.29), with a Chairperson (s.30) and a Director-General as chief executive (s.36). It is not a Ministry; it reports to the Minister assigned the subject of data protection (s.47–48) and its accounts are audited under Article 154 of the Constitution (s.42).

## What it exists to do (s.31)
Regulate the processing of personal data; safeguard privacy against the adverse effects of digitalisation in the public and private sectors; provide mechanisms protecting people in digital transactions; and ensure compliance so as to facilitate growth and innovation in the digital economy.

## What it can do now (ss.32–35, in force)
- Inspect information held by controllers and processors and direct them to comply (s.32(b)–(c)).
- Receive complaints, conduct inquiries, summon people and examine them on oath (s.32(e)–(f)); enter premises and seize records where processing poses an imminent risk (s.32(g)).
- After an inquiry, issue a **binding directive** requiring a controller or processor to stop, to rectify, or to pay compensation to a person who suffered harm (s.35(2)). This is the DPA's core enforcement tool today.
- Make rules, issue guidelines and directives (s.32(s), s.51A); recognise certification bodies; set standards (s.32(j), (q)); appoint sectoral advisory committees (s.33(o)); make rules on special categories, profiling, automated decisions and marketing messages (s.33(p)); and licence identity-management services (s.34).
- Direct controllers to comply with the transparency and access provisions (s.33(a)).

Note: the DPA has stated it will only investigate complaints and hear appeals once the governing provisions are in operation (Circular 01/2026 para 2.2). Part II appeals (s.19) and Part VII penalties (s.38) are not in force.

## What it cannot yet do
- Impose administrative penalties (Part VII — not in force).
- Decide data-subject appeals under s.19 (Part II — not in force).

## What it has published (as at 31 Oct 2026)
- Circular No. 01/2026 (7 Aug 2026) on the Act's application in the public sector, superseding Circular 01/2024.
- Seven draft rules/regulations/directives for consultation (Sep–Oct 2024) — none finalised.
- Its own Data Protection Statement (updated 20 Aug 2026), which also confirms the commencement position.
- No determinations, directives against named controllers, or annual reports have been found in public sources (OQ-13).

## Rules and regulations — how they are made
- The Authority makes **rules** (s.52) on, among other things, the form and manner of exercising data-subject rights and refusal criteria; rules on those matters require public consultation of at least two weeks, the Minister's approval, Gazette publication, and approval by Parliament within three months. First rules were required within 36 months of Part V's commencement (as amended).
- The Minister makes **regulations** (s.53) with the Authority's concurrence; they take effect on Gazette publication and must be approved by Parliament within three months.
- The Authority issues **guidelines** (s.51A, inserted 2025) and **directives** (s.33(c), (e)).

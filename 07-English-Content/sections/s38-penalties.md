> ⚠️ **This provision is not yet in force.** No start date has been set. Read section 9 below before relying on anything here.

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> (1) Where a controller or processor fails to comply with a directive issued under s.35, the Authority — considering the impact on data subjects, the nature and extent of the non-compliance and the s.39 factors — may by notice require payment of a penalty "which shall not exceed a sum of rupees ten million for each non-compliance". (2) A controller or processor previously penalised who again fails to conform to a directive is liable to an additional penalty of twice the amount imposed on the second and each subsequent occasion. (3) Penalties go to the Consolidated Fund after deducting compensation ordered under s.35(2)(c). (4) Unpaid penalties are recoverable through the Magistrate's Court of Colombo. (5) Other regulators may still take their own action (licence suspension etc.). (6) Directors and officers responsible for management (or partners) are personally liable unless they prove no knowledge or due diligence. (7) Appeal to the Court of Appeal within 21 working days; (9) with a cash deposit equal to the penalty; (10) the burden of proof lies on the controller/processor. — s.38

## 2. In plain English

The fine is up to ten million rupees — but it is not a fine for the breach itself. The Authority must first investigate and issue a directive (s.35, already in force); the penalty is for **ignoring that directive**. Repeat failures double. Directors can be personally liable. Appeals go to the Court of Appeal, but you must deposit the full amount first and prove you complied.

## 4. What it means in practice

Three widespread misstatements to avoid: 'Rs 10 million for a data breach' (wrong — it is per non-compliance with a directive); 'fines apply now' (Part VII is not in force); 'the DPA can fine foreign companies directly' (enforcement abroad is practically untested). Compensation to affected individuals is a separate s.35(2)(c) directive power that *is* in force.

## 5. Who is affected

Every controller and processor; directors, partners and managers personally (s.38(6)).

## 6. Everyday example

A company ignores an Authority directive to stop selling customer lists. When Part VII is in force, the Authority may impose up to Rs 10 million; if it ignores a second directive, up to Rs 20 million on top.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Treat any s.35 directive as a board-level matter; compliance with directives is what the penalty regime tests.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

None at present — the section is not in force. Once commenced, the amounts above apply.

## 9. Current legal status

**NOT IN FORCE — no commencement date has been set.** This section was scheduled to commence on 18 March 2025 by Gazette 2366/08, but that date was repealed by Gazette 2427/34 on 14 March 2025 and no new Order has been made. The Data Protection Authority's own Data Protection Statement (updated 20 August 2026) confirms that Part II rights and Part VII penalties are not yet in force and "cannot be exercised right now". Everything on this page describes what the law *will* say once the Minister appoints a date by Gazette. It creates no present entitlement or liability.

# Section 39 — Factors in setting a penalty

Part VII: Penalties (ss.38–40) · Status: **NOT IN FORCE — no date set**

> ⚠️ **This provision is not yet in force.** No start date has been set. Read section 9 below before relying on anything here.

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> In determining a penalty the Authority shall have regard to: (a) nature, gravity and duration of the contravention, scope and purpose of processing, number of data subjects and level of damage; (b) mitigation action taken; (c) effectiveness of the Data Protection Management Programme under s.12; (d) co-operation with the Authority; (e) categories of data affected; (f) how the contravention became known, in particular whether the controller/processor notified it; (g) previous non-compliances; (h) other aggravating or mitigating factors such as financial benefit gained or loss avoided. — s.39

## 2. In plain English

If a penalty is ever imposed, how much depends on how bad and how long the problem was, whether you had a real compliance programme, whether you co-operated and self-reported, and whether you profited.

## 4. What it means in practice

This is the strongest practical argument for investing in a DPMP (s.12) and a breach-notification process (s.23) before 2027: both are explicit mitigating factors.

## 5. Who is affected

Controllers and processors facing enforcement.

## 6. Everyday example

Two companies suffer the same breach. One has a documented DPMP, reports it promptly and co-operates; the other hides it. Section 39 points to a far lower penalty for the first.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Keep evidence of your DPMP, training, incident log and co-operation.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

None at present — the section is not in force. Once commenced, these factors determine the amount under s.38.

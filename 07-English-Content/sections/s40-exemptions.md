# Section 40 — Exemptions, restrictions and derogations

Part VII: Penalties (ss.38–40) · Status: **NOT IN FORCE — no date set**

> ⚠️ **This provision is not yet in force.** No start date has been set. Read section 9 below before relying on anything here.

## 1. What the law says (referenced extract)

> No exemption, restriction or derogation from the Act is allowed except where provided for in a law, respects the essence of fundamental rights and freedoms, and is a necessary and proportionate measure in a democratic society for: (a) national security, defence, public safety, public health, economic and financial stability; (b) impartiality and independence of the judiciary; (c) prevention, investigation and prosecution of criminal offences; (d) execution of criminal penalties; (e) protection of the rights and freedoms of persons, particularly freedom of expression and the right to information. — s.40

## 2. In plain English

The Act cannot be switched off by a contract or a policy. Only another law can carve out exceptions, and only for listed purposes (security, courts, crime, public health, free speech and RTI), and only where necessary and proportionate.

## 4. What it means in practice

Important for journalists, researchers and RTI users: s.40(e) is the Act's acknowledgement that data protection must coexist with freedom of expression and the Right to Information Act. It also governs how far police and security agencies can rely on other statutes.

## 5. Who is affected

Public authorities; law enforcement; media; everyone relying on an exemption.

## 6. Everyday example

A newspaper publishes a public official's name in a corruption story. The official's personal data is involved, but s.40(e) recognises that a law protecting freedom of expression can justify a proportionate derogation.

## 7. What to consider doing

- Do not assume an exemption exists; identify the written law that provides it and test it against s.40.

## 8. Consequences of not complying

Not a penalty section; it limits how exemptions may be claimed.

# Podcast / audio-overview briefs (NotebookLM)

Use NotebookLM's audio overview with the sources listed in PRODUCTION-PACK-README.md. Paste the "steer" text as the customisation prompt. Target 8–12 minutes each. Record Sinhala episodes separately with a Sinhala-speaking host or a reviewed script — do not auto-translate.

## E01 — "So, is it actually in force?"
Sources: COMMENCEMENT-STATUS.md; faq.md; the four Gazettes (English).
Steer: Two hosts for a general Sri Lankan audience. Explain that the Act is partly in force, what started in 2023, what starts 1 January 2027, and what has no date (Parts II, IV, VII). Explain why the 18 March 2025 date was cancelled. Never claim the Act is fully in force. Avoid legal jargon; when a section number is mentioned, say what it does in one sentence.

## E02 — "The kade owner's guide"
Sources: small-business-guide.md; small-business-checklist.md; scenarios 01, 03, 10, 11, 12.
Steer: One host is a small-shop owner asking practical questions, the other explains. Cover phone numbers, the counter notice, keeping the book safe, CCTV signs, WhatsApp orders, what to do if a phone is stolen. State clearly that no breach deadline exists yet and that most shops will not need a DPO. Keep it reassuring and concrete.

## E03 — "Your rights — and when you'll get them"
Sources: citizen-pathway.md; sections s13–s19; faq.md.
Steer: Walk through each right in Part II, with an everyday example for each, and repeat that none is in force yet. Explain what a person can do today (ask anyway; complain to the Authority). Do not invent timelines.

## E04 — "Children's data: tuition classes and schools"
Sources: scenarios 04 and 05; s05-lawful-processing.md; glossary.
Steer: Explain that data about under-16s is a special category, parental consent, results posted in WhatsApp groups, biometric attendance and DPIAs. Audience: parents and tuition teachers.

## E05 — "The 2025 amendment, explained"
Sources: draft-instrument-summaries.md; LEGAL-DOCUMENT-REGISTER.md; Amendment Act PDF.
Steer: For professionals. What changed: phased commencement, one-month responses, outsourced DPOs, the new cross-border section, public corporations and DPOs, the new guidelines power. Explain which 2024 drafts are now stranded.
